Model and limits

  • IDT: impulse (crossed-field) model, uniform single-electrode IDT, metallization ratio 0.5. Ga = 8K²CsWf₀Np²(sin x/x)², Ba its Hilbert transform, Ct = NpCsW. No electrode reflections, mass loading, diffraction or bulk-wave loss.
  • Delay line: two IDTs with the same aperture and period; acoustic transfer √(Ga₁Ga₂)·sinc·sinc·e−jωτ. With regeneration on, the exact Y→S conversion includes the triple-transit echo. Group delay is left blank across transmission zeros and where |S21| is more than 40 dB below its peak.
  • Heavy electrodes: the SAW is slower under the IDTs than on the free surface (mass loading and shorting). The model does not compute this shift; enter it as Δv/v₀ (for example from FEM or a measured resonance). The IDT period, electrode pitch and transit time inside the IDTs then use v₀(1 + Δv/v₀); the gap uses v₀.
  • Ports: probe (Z₀) → optional tuning L → pad (shunt Cpad) → lead (R, L) → finger resistance (2/3)ρW/(a h Np) → IDT. Feedthrough Cft couples the two pad nodes.
  • Substrate: a partial-wave solver for the piezoelectric half-space gives the free and metallized SAW velocities v₀ and vₘ, K² = 2(v₀ − vₘ)/v₀, the effective permittivity ε∞ (used as Cs) and the power-flow angle, for any cut and direction. LiNbO₃ constants: Andrushchak et al. (2009, sample smp-1) with ρ = 4700 kg/m³ by default, or Kushibiki et al. (1999). Rayleigh-type waves only: orientations with leaky or pseudo-SAW (e.g. 41° and 64° Y-cuts, X propagation) are not modelled. Published values for Y-Z and 128°Y at φ = 0 (Datta 1986) are shown for comparison. Propagation angle φ: on 128°Y and rotated Y-cuts, φ = 0 is the X direction, positive counter-clockwise about the outward surface normal.
  • Pads: Cpad from a coplanar-waveguide estimate; replace it with a value from an open-pad dummy if you have one.
